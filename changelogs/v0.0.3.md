# v0.0.3

## Fixed

**Connecting to a monitor after enabling its heart rate broadcast mode.**
Android caches a peripheral's list of Bluetooth services against its MAC
address, independent of what the device currently offers. If you connected to a
monitor once *before* switching on its broadcast mode, Android kept handing back
that stale, heart-rate-less list forever — so the app kept reporting that the
device wasn't exposing the Heart Rate Service even after you'd turned the mode
on. The connection now asks Android to refresh that cache, so the device's
current services are what get read.

This is the other half of the v0.0.2 connection fix. That one corrected the
app's own discovery bug; this one clears the stale cache underneath it.

## Added

**Touch a chart to read it.** Press and drag anywhere on a heart rate chart —
live or historical — and a readout follows your finger showing the BPM and the
time at that point. Lift to dismiss.

You don't have to hit the line. The readout snaps to whichever point is nearest
your finger, so a rough touch anywhere in the chart still lands on something: a
fingertip covers about 44 pixels, the point markers are 4.5.

A dashed crosshair marks where you are, and the point under it is enlarged, so
it's clear which reading the numbers belong to. Near the start or end of a
session the readout shifts inward instead of running off the edge.

Dragging vertically still scrolls the page, so the chart doesn't trap the
gesture.

## Under the hood

- `indexAtX` and `clampReadoutX` join the other chart geometry in
  `src/utils/chart.ts` as pure functions, tested directly: the round trip
  through `scale.x` and back, nearest-point snapping either side of the
  midpoint, clamping outside the plot area, single-point and empty series, and
  readout overflow at both edges.
- `PanResponder` rather than `react-native-gesture-handler`. GH is already a
  dependency, but `react-native-reanimated` is not, and GH v2 falling back to
  JS-thread callbacks is an untested combination on RN 0.83's new architecture
  that could not be verified without a device. The gesture here is a
  single-touch x read, so none of GH's advantages apply.
- `onPanResponderTerminationRequest` returns true, so the parent scroll view can
  claim a vertical drag; the readout is cleared on terminate as well as release,
  or it strands on screen every time that happens.
- The active index is re-validated against the current series each render. The
  live series can shrink across a downsample boundary between the touch and the
  render, and a stale index would dereference undefined.
- The readout shows BPM and time only. The interval's range is already visible
  as the shaded band, and the live-vs-baseline delta is on the card above.
- The live chart keeps recording and appending during a scrub, so the reading
  under your finger can shift as the axis rescales. Freezing the view would mean
  holding and reconciling a copy of the series; the live BPM card directly above
  already shows the current value.

**Known limitation.** Per-point BPM on a historical chart is now reachable only
by scrubbing — there is no table view. Session average and max remain on the
detail screen and the axis ticks stay, but the guidance this project follows
holds that a tooltip should enhance rather than gate. Worth a follow-up.

- `connectToDevice` passes `refreshGatt: 'OnConnected'` to ble-plx, forcing
  Android to re-read the peripheral's GATT service list instead of serving its
  MAC-keyed cache. Without it, `assertHeartRateService` rejects a device whose
  broadcast mode was enabled after the first connection, permanently.
- `docs/releasing.md` corrected: the repository is public, not private. The
  in-app update checker calls the GitHub releases API unauthenticated — no token
  ships in the app — and that API returns 404 for a private repo to avoid
  leaking its existence, indistinguishable from "no releases yet". That made
  every update check fail while the repo was private. The doc now records why it
  must stay public, or what would have to change if it doesn't.

159 unit tests pass, up from 149; typecheck is clean.
